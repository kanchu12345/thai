When to Travel

Sri Lanka Wildlife Safari Seasons

Thanks to dual monsoons, Sri Lanka offers world-class safari and marine wildlife viewings 365 days a year. Here is your seasonal planning guide:

☀️

November – April

Southern & Western Safaris

The premier season for southern parks and marine giants. Calm, crystalline waters in the south allow daily private whale-watching charters in Mirissa, while dry sunny conditions in Yala and Bundala optimize predator and migratory bird sightings.

  • Yala National Park: Optimal leopard activity & dry waterhole viewing.
  • Mirissa Ocean: Peak Blue Whale, Sperm Whale & spinner dolphin migrations.
  • Bundala Wetland: Thousands of migratory flamingos, storks & waders.
🌿

May – October

North-Central & Eastern Parks

As the southwest receives rains, the north-central and eastern dry zones bask in bright sunshine. This period witnesses the globally celebrated Asian Elephant Gathering at Minneriya and superb bear sightings in Wilpattu.

  • The Great Gathering: Up to 300 wild elephants grazing by Minneriya tank.
  • Wilpattu National Park: Palu fruit season brings sloth bears onto roads.
  • Trincomalee Bay: Calm northeastern seas with Blue Whale sightings.

Safari Essentials

Wildlife Travel FAQ

Sri Lanka has the world's highest density of leopards, particularly in Yala Block 1 and Wilpattu. While sightings can never be 100% guaranteed in the wild, our guests who take at least 2 to 3 game drives (dawn and late afternoon) have over an 85% success rate of photographing leopards in their natural habitats.

We provide private, customized 4x4 Toyota Hilux or Land Cruiser safari jeeps equipped with tiered raised seating, canopy sunshades, cushioned seats, charging ports, and open sides designed specifically for 360-degree wildlife viewing and photography.

We recommend wearing neutral, earthy-toned lightweight cotton clothing (khaki, olive, beige, or tan) to avoid startling animals. Bring wide-brimmed sun hats, high-SPF sunscreen, polarized sunglasses, insect repellent, and your telephoto zoom lens (200mm–400mm is ideal for wildlife photography).
